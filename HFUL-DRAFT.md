## 1. Purpose and scope

The Horizon Fair Use License (HFUL) is intended to let individuals, students, hobbyists, independent developers and studios use Horizon Engine without upfront engine fees or subscriptions. A royalty is proposed only when an individual game made with Horizon Engine exceeds the revenue threshold in Section 4.

HFUL applies only to Horizon Engine code and materials for which the person or entity issuing the licence has the necessary copyright and licensing rights. Third-party components remain governed by their own licences.

## 2. Definitions

**Engine** means the Horizon Engine software and the accompanying materials expressly distributed under HFUL.

**Game** means one separately identifiable game product developed using the Engine, including its ports, editions, updates, expansions, downloadable content and in-game transactions. Revenue must not be artificially split between editions, platforms, publishers or related entities to avoid the threshold.

**Lifetime Net Game Revenue** means cumulative revenue actually received by the developer, publisher or their affiliates from commercial exploitation of that Game, less only:
- sales, value-added and similar transaction taxes collected and remitted to a government authority;
- refunds and chargebacks; and
- third-party storefront, platform and payment-processing commissions actually withheld.

It does not deduct development costs, salaries, marketing, general overhead, financing costs or income taxes. Advertising, subscriptions, bundles, licensing, in-game purchases and other receipts attributable to the Game are included. Shared or bundle receipts must be allocated to the Game on a reasonable, consistently applied basis.

**Threshold** means US$300,000 in cumulative Lifetime Net Game Revenue for each Game.

## 3. Free use below the threshold

Use of the Engine is royalty-free while a Game's cumulative Lifetime Net Game Revenue is at or below the Threshold. No upfront fee, subscription or payment is due merely to download, install, modify or use the Engine.

Free, educational, personal and non-commercial projects do not owe royalties unless they later generate revenue that counts as Lifetime Net Game Revenue.

## 4. Royalty after the threshold

Once a Game's cumulative Lifetime Net Game Revenue exceeds the Threshold, the licensee owes a royalty equal to **4% of the portion of cumulative Lifetime Net Game Revenue above US$300,000**.

The royalty is calculated on the excess only, not on the first US$300,000 and not retroactively on revenue below the Threshold.

**Formula:** Royalty = 4% × max(0, Lifetime Net Game Revenue − US$300,000)

Examples:
- US$300,000 revenue: US$0 royalty.
- US$400,000 revenue: US$4,000 royalty.
- US$500,000 revenue: US$8,000 royalty.
- US$1,000,000 revenue: US$28,000 royalty.
- US$5,000,000 revenue: US$188,000 royalty.

The Threshold is measured separately for each Game. Revenue from unrelated games is not aggregated.

## 5. Records, reports and payments

A licensee whose Game exceeds the Threshold must keep reasonably complete records sufficient to calculate Lifetime Net Game Revenue and the royalty.

Within 45 days after the end of each calendar quarter in which a royalty is due, the licensee must provide a report showing the calculation and pay the amount due. Reports are not required for a Game that has not exceeded the Threshold.

The licensor may, no more than once in a 12-month period and on at least 30 days' written notice, have an independent professional bound by confidentiality review records reasonably necessary to verify the report. The review must be conducted during normal business hours and in a manner that minimises disruption. The licensor bears the review cost unless an underpayment of more than 5% for the reviewed period is found, in which case the licensee must reimburse reasonable review costs.

Amounts are payable in US dollars or, where payment in US dollars is not reasonably available, the equivalent converted using a publicly available exchange rate on the payment date. Any legally required withholding must be documented.

The Engine and its original components remain owned by their respective copyright holders. A licensee retains ownership of their independently created game, source code, artwork, music, story and other original content, subject to any third-party rights.

Nothing in HFUL transfers ownership of a Game to Horizon or grants Horizon a right to publish, sell or modify a licensee's Game.

## 8. Third-party components and contributions

Third-party libraries, assets and tools included with or used alongside the Engine are not relicensed by HFUL. Their notices and licence terms must be preserved and followed.

Contributions are not automatically covered by HFUL merely because they are submitted to a repository. The project must obtain the contributor permissions needed before distributing those contributions under HFUL.

## 9. Term and termination

The licence begins when the licensee accepts it or exercises rights under a release expressly offered under HFUL.

A material breach may result in termination only after written notice describing the breach and a reasonable 30-day opportunity to cure it, where the breach can be cured. Termination does not transfer ownership of the licensee's Game or remove rights in copies already lawfully distributed to end users under separate game distribution terms.

Any accrued royalty and reporting obligations for periods before termination survive termination.

This limitation does not apply where prohibited by law or to liability that cannot legally be limited.

## 12. Changes to the licence

A release must identify the exact HFUL version that governs it. A later version does not automatically change the terms of an earlier release. No change may retroactively increase royalties for revenue already earned under an earlier accepted version.
