## 1. Purpose and Scope

The Horizon Engine Revenue License (HERL) establishes the terms under which individuals, students, hobbyists, independent developers, businesses and studios may use, modify and distribute Horizon Engine.

HERL is designed around the following principles:

1. No upfront licensing fees.
2. No mandatory subscription.
3. Royalty-free development and use below the applicable revenue threshold.
4. A limited royalty on successful commercial games exceeding that threshold.
5. Developers retain ownership of their independently created games and content.
6. Permission to distribute commercial games containing Horizon Engine.

HERL applies only to software, documentation and other materials that the Licensor is legally authorized to distribute under this license.

Third-party software and materials remain subject to their respective licenses.

## 2. Definitions

For purposes of HERL:

### 2.1 Engine

"Engine" means the Horizon Engine software, including source code, compiled binaries, editor components, official tools, documentation and other materials explicitly identified as being distributed under HERL.

### 2.2 Licensee

"Licensee" means the individual or legal entity that accepts HERL and exercises rights granted under it.

Where multiple parties independently accept HERL, each party is responsible for its own obligations unless a separate written agreement establishes shared responsibility.

### 2.3 Licensor

"Licensor" means the copyright owner or authorized licensing entity identified in the applicable release or license notice.

### 2.4 Game

"Game" means one separately identifiable interactive entertainment product developed using the Engine.

A Game includes its commercially related:

- Platform ports.
- Editions and regional releases.
- Updates and patches.
- Downloadable content (DLC).
- Expansions.
- In-game purchases and transactions.
- Advertising and subscription revenue.
- Other commercial extensions that form part of the same product.

Revenue must not be artificially divided between platforms, editions, publishers, related entities or distribution channels to avoid the applicable threshold.

A sequel, remake, remaster or standalone expansion may be treated as a separate Game only when it is genuinely a separately identifiable product rather than an artificial subdivision of an existing Game.

### 2.5 Lifetime Net Game Revenue

"Lifetime Net Game Revenue" means the cumulative revenue actually received by the Licensee, its affiliates, or publishers and distributors acting in connection with the commercial exploitation of a Game.

Only the following amounts may be deducted:

1. Sales taxes, value-added taxes and similar transaction taxes collected and remitted to government authorities.
2. Refunds and chargebacks actually issued.
3. Third-party storefront, platform and payment-processing commissions actually withheld.

The following expenses are not deductible:

- Development costs.
- Employee salaries and contractor payments.
- Marketing and advertising expenses.
- General operating expenses.
- Office and equipment expenses.
- Financing costs.
- Income taxes.
- General business overhead.

Revenue includes advertising, subscriptions, bundles, licensing, sponsorships, in-game purchases and other commercial receipts attributable to the Game.

Where a bundle or subscription includes multiple products, revenue must be allocated using a reasonable, consistent and documented method.

Revenue received in a foreign currency must be converted into United States dollars using a publicly available exchange rate on the date the relevant revenue is received, or another consistently applied method agreed to in writing by the Licensor.

### 2.6 Revenue Threshold

"Revenue Threshold" means **US$300,000 in cumulative Lifetime Net Game Revenue per Game**.

### 2.7 Royalty Rate

"Royalty Rate" means **4% of the portion of Lifetime Net Game Revenue exceeding the Revenue Threshold**.

### 2.8 Affiliate

"Affiliate" means an entity that controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with the Licensee.

## 5. Commercial Game Royalty

### 5.1 Royalty Obligation

When a Game exceeds US$300,000 in cumulative Lifetime Net Game Revenue, the Licensee becomes responsible for a royalty equal to 4% of the revenue exceeding that threshold.

The royalty is not calculated on the first US$300,000.

Revenue below the threshold is never retroactively charged.

### 5.2 Calculation

The cumulative royalty liability is calculated as:

**Cumulative Royalty Liability = 4% × max(0, Lifetime Net Game Revenue − US$300,000)**

### 5.3 Examples

| Lifetime Net Game Revenue | Cumulative Royalty Liability |
|---:|---:|
| $100,000 | $0 |
| $300,000 | $0 |
| $400,000 | $4,000 |
| $500,000 | $8,000 |
| $1,000,000 | $28,000 |
| $2,000,000 | $68,000 |
| $5,000,000 | $188,000 |

### 5.4 Separate Game Thresholds

The Revenue Threshold applies separately to each Game.

Revenue from unrelated Games must not be combined to reach the threshold.

Related editions, ports and commercial extensions of the same Game cannot be artificially separated to obtain multiple thresholds.

### 5.5 No Retroactive Royalty

The Licensee owes no royalty on the first US$300,000 of Lifetime Net Game Revenue.

The royalty applies only to the portion exceeding that amount.

## 6. Royalty Reporting and Payment

### 6.1 Reporting Requirement

Once a Game exceeds the Revenue Threshold, the Licensee must maintain reasonably complete financial records sufficient to calculate the royalty.

The Licensee must submit a quarterly report within 45 days after the end of each calendar quarter.

The report must include:

1. The Game's cumulative Lifetime Net Game Revenue.
2. Applicable deductions.
3. Currency conversion calculations, where relevant.
4. The cumulative royalty liability.
5. Royalties previously paid.
6. The remaining payment due.

A Game that has never exceeded the threshold does not require royalty reporting.

Once the threshold has been exceeded, reporting continues for subsequent quarters in which a royalty payment, adjustment or credit remains relevant.

### 6.2 Quarterly Payment Calculation

Previously paid royalties must always be credited against the cumulative royalty liability.

The amount payable for a reporting period is:

**Current Payment Due = Current Cumulative Royalty Liability − Previously Paid Royalties − Applicable Credits**

For example:

| Quarter | Cumulative Revenue | Cumulative Liability | New Payment Due |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Q1 | $400,000 | $4,000 | $4,000 |
| Q2 | $500,000 | $8,000 | $4,000 |
| Q3 | $600,000 | $12,000 | $4,000 |
| Q4 | $700,000 | $16,000 | $4,000 |

The Licensee must not be charged the full cumulative liability repeatedly.

If refunds or chargebacks reduce cumulative Lifetime Net Game Revenue, the resulting adjustment must be reflected in the next report. Any resulting overpayment must be credited against future royalties or refunded if no further royalty is payable.

### 6.3 Payment Currency

Payments must be made in United States dollars unless the parties agree otherwise in writing.

Any legally required withholding must be documented by the Licensee.

### 6.4 Payment Information

### 6.5 Late Payments
